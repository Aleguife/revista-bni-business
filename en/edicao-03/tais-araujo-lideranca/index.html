Taís Araújo traded six promotions at Itaú for clarity of purpose — and now certifies leaders through the method that transformed her own journey

Two job offers were on the table. Both offered a fixed salary, benefits and the security many professionals seek after leaving a large company.

By Fernanda Sodré

After building a 14-year career at Itaú, with six promotions and ending her path as general manager of a branch network, Taís Araújo had every reason to remain in the financial market.

She turned down both offers.

The decision surprised many people, but it made complete sense to those who knew her story.

“I would never have had the courage to make that choice if I had not first developed self-confidence. I learned that there is a difference between financial security and clarity of purpose.” Now an internationally certified Dale Carnegie trainer and speaker, her decision was not impulsive. It came from a question asked by Marcelo Filippin, Commercial Director at Dale Carnegie, in a conversation about her career goals: “What are your goals for the next five years?” Taís could not answer at the time. But the question stayed with her.

Long before her career, there was a story

Raised between São Paulo and the countryside of Ceará, Taís grew up amid constant change, instability and family challenges that deeply marked her childhood.

She arrived in São Paulo at 16 with few resources, but with an immense willingness to build a different life.

She worked as a nanny, domestic worker, waitress, attendant and cashier. Often, work also meant having a place to live. There were times when she had to move house repeatedly, without knowing where she would go next.

Those experiences could have defined her limits.

Instead, they defined her strength.

“Today I understand that my story was not a sentence. It was preparing me for who I would become.”

A journey built one role at a time

While working, Taís found in education an opportunity to transform her circumstances.

She completed a Business Administration degree through the Escola da Família program, an initiative that offered scholarships in exchange for volunteer work on social projects at weekends.

Later, while already at Itaú, she earned a scholarship for a postgraduate program in Strategic Marketing at Universidade Presbiteriana Mackenzie. She also invested in training in Neuro-Linguistic Programming (NLP), human development and leadership, making learning a lasting commitment.

In 2011, she joined Itaú as a cashier. Over 14 years, she moved through different roles, from customer service and relationship manager to general manager of a branch network. There were six promotions built on performance, ongoing development and the ability to lead people. Each advance taught me something I use in the training room today: “The role may change. But if you do not change with it, you get stuck.”

When purpose spoke louder

Her first contact with the Dale Carnegie methodology came while she was still working at the bank.

The training triggered something she did not expect: a personal transformation even before a professional one.

“Before learning leadership techniques, I needed to learn to lead myself.”

Through that process, she found clarity about the next chapter of her life.

When she ended her time at Itaú, she chose to give up stability and devote herself fully to human development, relinquishing a fixed salary to work exclusively on commission. That was the context in which she declined the two offers with guaranteed pay: she had clearly identified that her purpose was developing people, not managing investment portfolios.

She completed international certification as a Dale Carnegie Trainer, a rigorous practical evaluation process conducted by Carnegie Master Brasil.

Leadership begins before the title

Today, Taís leads training in self-confidence, communication and leadership, working both with groups of professionals from different sectors and with customized in-company projects for each client’s specific needs. Her executive experience lets her unite theory and practice. She has led teams, managed results, faced difficult decisions, conducted complex feedback conversations and lived the challenges she now discusses within companies. For her, that hands-on experience in leadership positions is what gives credibility to what she teaches — more than any certification.

“I do not teach only concepts. I share experiences I have lived in practice.”

Among the results she cites is closing a major corporate contract after a diagnostic process with the board of a company with nine partners — a relationship that continued after the training ended, including invitations to speak to more than 1,000 employees at institutional events.

Communication: the skill that transforms careers

For Taís, communication is the competency that sustains every other one.

“A large part of the challenges we see in companies does not come from a lack of technical knowledge. It arises from the difficulty of listening, influencing, building trust and creating healthy relationships.”

She believes developing people is much more than teaching tools.

It is helping someone discover a better version of themselves.

For her, many leadership problems she observes in companies are not caused by a lack of technical knowledge, but by difficulty communicating ideas clearly, listening actively and adapting the message to different audiences — from frontline employees to executive directors. It was exactly that gap, she recalls, that a manager pointed out early in her banking career: she sold well, but her reactive communication compromised her credibility as a professional.

Leadership is not a title. It is practice.

For Taís, one of the most common mistakes in the corporate environment is treating hierarchical position as synonymous with readiness. “You can own a company, manage a large bank, and that does not mean you are ready. There is always something to develop,” she says. This conviction guides her work as a speaker: discussing leadership blind spots — what an executive cannot see alone, but what compromises their ability to influence and retain talent.

The combination of practical experience in leadership roles and technical training in organizational behavior sustains Taís Araújo’s current position: a professional who builds authority through concrete experience — someone who has led teams, made difficult career decisions and now devotes her work to preparing other executives to do the same.

Rewriting one’s own story

Today, alongside her corporate work, Taís is developing an original project that brings together her journey, executive experience and vision of leadership.

Her keynote “The identity that sustains the next level of leadership” begins with the conviction that no one is defined by their past, but by the decisions they choose to make from it.

That same concept will give rise to her first book.

“I believe the greatest leadership a person can exercise is over their own life. Because when someone transforms themselves, they gain the authority to transform other people.”

Looking at her journey, Taís realizes that her greatest achievement was not a title, promotion or professional recognition.

It was discovering that no experience is wasted when it finds a purpose.

Her story is still being written.

Now, helping others rewrite theirs.
